## Part B — What the Clients & Partners page needs

Here's our honest position, and we'd rather say it plainly than build something that undersells you.

A page titled **Clients & Partners** sets a specific expectation. A prospective client — particularly a procurement team at a large organisation — opens it to answer one question: *who has this firm actually worked with, and who do they deliver through?*

The industry list answers a different question: *what sectors do they work in?* That's valuable, but your website already answers it on the home page and the About page. If we built the Clients & Partners page from the industry list alone, a visitor would click it expecting evidence and find a list they'd already read — which would work against you on the one page where credibility matters most.

**To build the page properly, we need at least one of the following three.**

### 1. Clients

Ideally named, with your confirmation that we may name them.

**If confidentiality prevents naming them, that is not a dead end.** Approved anonymous descriptions work well and are standard practice for firms in your sectors. For example:

> *"A UK nuclear new-build operator"*
> *"A Tier-1 highways contractor"*
> *"An offshore energy operator in West Africa"*

These carry real weight without breaching any agreement. We'd need you to approve the exact wording of each.

### 2. Partners

Your website already states — in four places — that you work through *"a network of technical partners and manufacturers across the United Kingdom, United States, Europe and China."* At the moment that claim isn't supported by anything a reader can check.

Naming even a few of those partners, with their permission, would turn a claim into evidence. Anonymous descriptions work here too.

### 3. Projects

For each project you're willing to reference:

- Sector
- Scope of work
- Lazfields' specific role
- Approximate dates
- Outcome or result

Two or three well-described projects are worth more than a long list of names.

### Also useful, if you have them

- Framework appointments or approved-supplier status
- Permission to use client or partner logos
- Testimonials, or referees willing to be contacted

## An alternative, if client names aren't available

If naming clients and partners isn't possible right now, there's a good second option: build the page as **Industries We Serve** instead.

That title is honest about what the page contains, and it can be genuinely useful — but only if each industry carries something a reader can't get from the home page. For each of your nine industries we'd need:

- **Two to four sentences** on what Lazfields actually delivers in that sector — typical scope, the kind of problems you're brought in to solve
- **Which of your six services** most apply there
- **Any sector-specific standards or qualifications** that govern the work

This requires no client names at all, and it would make a strong page. Without it, we'd just be repeating the sector list a third time.
